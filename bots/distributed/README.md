# distributed: cooperation from local views

**Question.** Which organization turns cooperation into completed production and conversion
when each champion sees only its own vision, and the team shares no memory and sends no
messages? **Shared hypothesis:** cooperation pays when every cooperative transition is licensed
by the acting champion's own current observation (never a predicted arrival, a teammate's
private state or elapsed time alone), and every organization keeps a named productive solo
fallback, so a missing partner changes speed, not viability.

## Doctrine

- **Held fixed** (shared modules): full-cycle accounting inherited from the doctrines suite
  (score comparison over complete clear, capture, bank and renewal cycles; conservative
  survival; fuel guards), personal observation and exposure bounds, forecasts and quotes, the
  legal executor and the production engine of `reference/src/policy.h`.
- **Varied per bot:** the shape of execution dependencies: who must personally see what before
  acting, who belongs to which crew and for how long, what the public clock prescribes, and what
  happens when a partner is missing or fails. That lives in the bot's `policy.h` /
  `organization.h` plus its five-pick roster in `profile.h`.
- **Information boundary:** zero live shared bytes. No bot uses the 8-byte team memory or the
  1024-byte draft memory; every actor recomputes draft geometry from public map data. An ally's
  bag, gold, readiness or target is known only while that ally is in the actor's own vision.
  Occupation progress is a private estimate; the actor's own payout is the completion witness.
- **Roles by pick ordinal:** picks 1-3 are the core; picks 4-5 (34 of the 40 pool maps draft 4
  or 5) produce independently unless the bot gives them a visible role.
- **Not this suite:** the economic doctrine itself (doctrines), geography of cycles (territory),
  adoption gated on an actor's own completed episodes (adaptive), opponent-facing commitments
  (commitments), predictive models (models), job decision rules (decision), engine event timing
  (events). A gain that applies to every organization alike (better economy, routes or combat)
  is not progress on this question; pursue it only as an organization-neutral common fix.
- **Measured state** (older sources, before this layout): technically clean (no faults, caught
  errors or fuel exhaustion), but every bot, reference included, lost every recorded game to
  `anchors/fundamentals`, with more deaths than the control arms; most mechanisms never fired
  natively. First job for each bot: make its mechanism fire in real games and pay off.

## Distinctness rules

1. A bot is its decision rule. Two bots are distinct only if, from the same state, a different
   actor does different work, or production continues after a different dependency failure.
   A new label, period, threshold, estimator or roster alone does not count: two bots that make
   the same choices count as one, so merge or redesign the later one.
2. Show it in games: the bot's own decision labels fire and its actions differ from both
   `reference` (pure independent production) and its own control arm. An active arm whose
   native events equal its control's is not yet a distinct bot.
3. Organization logic lives only in `<bot>/src/`. `common/` and `reference/` hold
   organization-neutral observation, accounting, forecasting, execution and production. The
   slug-keyed branches in `common/src/runtime.h` and `runtime_joint.h` (caravan, owner-builder,
   replicas, firebrigade, bridgecrew, public-modes, launchpad) are bot logic in shared code: add
   no more, and move one behind a policy hook (like `verification_active`) when touching it.
4. Rosters keep each mechanism's anchor roles but must not repeat: caravan = checkpoints and
   launchpad = recovery-relay draft identically; trailhead/mesh and bridgecrew/public-modes
   draft the same five in other orders, as do guilds, contingencies, salvage and launchpad
   (Varg, Willow, Tavi, Marcella, Vendetta); Varg is the first pick of nine bots.
5. `common/` and `reference/` have one owner at a time (agreed with the integrator). A shared
   change moves all 17 bots: rebuild the suite, run its tests, and re-measure every affected
   bot before claiming an effect. Never tune a shared default to help one bot.
6. Never include another suite. `adaptive` keeps its own frozen copy of `contract.h` and
   `observe.h` (`bots/adaptive/distributed-common/`); changes here need no sync.

## Layout and checks

- `<bot>/src/main.cpp`: `p6::runtime::run<Policy>()`; `profile.h`: slug and roster;
  `policy.h`: `State` plus `choose(state, input, control)`. owner-builder, replicas and
  firebrigade keep a pure `policy.h` and bind it through `organization.h` (`ExecutablePolicy`).
- `common/src/`: `contract.h` (accounting), `observe.h` (personal frame, exposure, cycle
  forecasts, quotes), `crew_forecast.h` (three-actor crew work), `execute.h` (project state,
  reference step, executor), `organization.h` (Input, Decision, Intent, independent fallback),
  `runtime.h` (SDK adapter: draft, observation, quotes, movement, combat, banking, shopping, fuel
  guards, turn-boundary error catch) and `runtime_*.h` helpers (catalogue, combat, economy,
  intercept, joint, observation, opportunities, threat, trace).
- `reference/src/policy.h` is included by `runtime.h`, so it is part of every bot.
- Control arm: knob `P6_CONTROL` (`#define P6_CONTROL 0` in `runtime.h`); build or play it with
  the override `P6_CONTROL=1` (arena knob overrides). All 16 control arms build.
- Build: `./arena.cmd build distributed` (17 bots, content-hash snapshots).
- Tests: `./arena.cmd test distributed`, or
  `./arena.cmd test distributed -- [bot ...] [--case NAME] [--list] [--no-syntax]`:
  25 pure WASI fixtures (`tests/plan6-<case>.cpp`, Release optimisation -O3 -DNDEBUG), 17
  `syntax-<bot>` compiles of each `main.cpp` against the pinned SDK, and `syntax-combat-sdk`
  (the SDK block of the runtime-combat fixture); about 25 s. Naming a bot runs the fixtures
  that reach its `src/`, the runtime fixtures and its syntax case. Fixtures do not exercise
  `runtime.h` itself; only games do.
- Logs: caught errors print `p6 <slug> error: ...`. Numeric trace lines start `p6` plus a
  record letter: `c` turn summaries (at most 512 per actor: round, actor id, stage, group,
  intent, phase, bag, gold, score, fuel and costs, and `reason_hash`, the 32-bit FNV-1a hash of
  the decision label), `p` model preparation, `n` crew quotes, `d` turnbacks. Draft, geometry and
  model work stop at fuel thresholds (30-70 k) and resume on a later turn; a build does not
  prove fuel safety, so recheck fuel on large 5-pick maps after runtime changes.
