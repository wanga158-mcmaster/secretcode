# Active surveyor

Bot 10 studies active information acquisition. It ranks executable source jobs
by a cashflow horizon, then admits one own-ward probe only when an optimistic
observation of a wholly unseen source can reverse that work choice, or an
unseen incumbent's respawn can invalidate it. Only the incumbent and one
challenger participate in the probe. This is not a financing-lot threshold,
conversion service program, work auction or repeated production circuit.

The roster is Tavi, Vesper, Stellar, Marcella and Varg. A bounded shortlist
contains at most two detailed source jobs, including useful harmless work.
The source merit discounts gold gradually as personal cash accumulates,
prices work and travel, crowding, private enemy arrival and a bag-sensitive
life reserve. Ordinary farming remains useful when no pivotal probe exists.
Tavi buys Monster Strength and Jungle Strength; Vesper Monster Focus and
Jungle Health; Stellar Monster Focus and Jungle Focus; Marcella Monster
Strength and Jungle Health; Varg Monster Strength and Jungle Strength.
Later Vesper/Stellar/Marcella buy Power Focus, Varg Power Health and Tavi
Power Strength. The actual SDK shop calls this same pure purchase policy,
queries current prices and legality, and batches field-to-shop trips.
Marcella's conversion gold is never available before actual tribute.

A probe first passes cheap fuel, cooldown and eligibility checks. Only nearby
wholly unseen sources qualify; seeing any member spawn already makes the
group lifecycle queryable. Two directed geometric placements are considered,
with at most two terrain visibility predictions. The first successful useful
prediction ends sampling if it loses no useful old coverage. Old owned ward
coverage of either alternative has an opportunity cost after subtracting
ordinary body vision. Known invalid terrain, observed allied wards and known
occupants are excluded. The geometric mask does not prove placement legality.
The owner round, life, channel, restrictions and recharge are checked again
before the single attempted action. False placement gets no success credit.
An attempt closes that decision epoch for ten rounds while useful work proceeds.

Successful replacement discards old camp, champion and effective-vision
snapshots, because replacing an old ward can remove knowledge. The adapter
re-queries the owner's current effective vision and live lists, and rebuilds
the entire bounded job shortlist. A newly hidden job returns to conservative
public estimates. The rebuild has a continuation reserve and checks its round
again at completion. Stale or incomplete refresh aborts work for that turn.
A fresh but inconclusive sample still invalidates old facts and selects useful
work from the authoritative replacement. An incomplete refresh returns no
source choice for the current turn; the SDK executor enters recovery, obtains
fresh observations next owner turn and can recall before ordinary production
resumes. This spends more than the free ward: losing the current action and
the recovery trip is a real economic cost. Newly revealed danger is checked
before continuing. A changed-choice log is observation-associated evidence;
the native witness must identify the new live fact to establish attribution.

The low-level executor locally reuses bounded metadata, routing, observed-dead
frontier memory, capture escape and combat legality from the suite's settled
executor. It contains none of 09's Service, 08's handshake or 07's Circuit.
The capture watchdog persists through empty enemy vision and cancelled policy
targets; actual departure, death or changed reward lifecycle resets it. An
expired contested capture performs bounded departure or useful blocker work.
Channels are preserved. Zero cargo cannot impose a banking state. Unexpected
errors are caught at the owner-turn boundary, log `error`, and yield.

`HEURISTICS_ENABLED=1` is retained as a healthy, distinct losing research
bundle. Explicit zero includes frozen common Fundamentals directly, without
new bookkeeping. This is not a strength improvement or a submission claim.
